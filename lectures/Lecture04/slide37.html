log(n!) grows like n log n
Try n = 8
8! =8×7×6×5×4×3×2×1
top half: each ≥ 4rest: each ≥ 1, ignore
8! ≥ 4 · 4 · 4 · 4 = 44
In general
n! ≥ (n/2)n/2
log(n!) ≥ (n/2) · log(n/2) = Ω(n log n)

Ω means “at least”: the lower-bound partner of O.

The verdict

Any comparison sort needs Ω(n log n) comparisons in the worst case.

Merge sort is optimal. No amount of cleverness beats n log n…

…unless we stop comparing.
