What we learned today
Takeaways
1

Three products, not four

Karatsuba gets ad + bc as s − ac − bd, so T(n) = 3T(n/2) + Θ(n) = Θ(n1.585), faster than grade school’s Θ(n2).

2

Follow the query

RANGE-MIN splits A[lo..hi] at mid and follows A[i..j] into the left half, the right half, or both. Each element of the query ends in a base case of its own.

3

Non-overlapping subproblems

Divide and conquer splits a problem into parts that share nothing. Each subproblem is solved once, and its answer is used once and forgotten.

4

Overlapping subproblems

When different problems share a subproblem, divide and conquer solves it again and again, and the calls can grow exponentially. Dynamic programming, in a later lecture, solves each one once.
