When subproblems overlap

shared by two problems, so solved more than once

A hypothetical problem

Problem P splits into Q and R, and both of them need subproblem U.

The recursion tree solves U twice, and X and Y three times each: 15 calls for 10 different subproblems.

Every extra level multiplies the copies, so the calls can grow exponentially while the different subproblems stay few.

Overlapping subproblems
Different problems contain the same subproblem. Divide and conquer solves it again every time it comes up.
