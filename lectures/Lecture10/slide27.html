Odd cycle means not bipartite
Lemma
If BFS finds an edge (u, v) with u.d = v.d, then G has an odd cycle, so G is not bipartite.
Proof
  1. Let u.d = v.d = k. Walking back along π, both u and v reach s in k steps.
  2. The two walks and the edge (u, v) close a loop of k + k + 1 edges: odd. Their shared part is walked twice, so dropping it still leaves an odd cycle.
  3. Colors alternate at every step. u and v are both k steps from s, so they get the same color, yet (u, v) is an edge. So no bicoloring: not bipartite.
u.d = y.d = 3. Walks u, t, w, s and y, x, w, s,
plus (u, y): 3 + 3 + 1 = 7 edges. Dashed (w, s)
is walked twice; without it, a 5-cycle: still odd.
