Counting sort on digit i
1COUNTING-SORT-DIGIT(A, B, n, i)
2    let C[0..9] be a new array
3    for v = 0 to 9                 ◃ clear
4        C[v] = 0
5    for j = 0 to n − 1             ◃ count
6        key = ⌊A[j] / 10i⌋ mod 10   // digit i
7        C[key] = C[key] + 1
8    for v = 1 to 9                 ◃ add up
9        C[v] = C[v] + C[v − 1]
10    for j = n − 1 downto 0         ◃ place
11        key = ⌊A[j] / 10i⌋ mod 10
12        C[key] = C[key] − 1
13        B[C[key]] = A[j]
14    for j = 0 to n − 1             ◃ copy back
15        A[j] = B[j]
Keys are digits

The key of A[j] is its digit i (lines 6 and 11), so k = 9: just 10 counters, however big the numbers are.

Whole numbers move

Line 13 places the whole number A[j] into B, not just its digit.

In RADIX-SORT

Line 5 of RADIX-SORT calls COUNTING-SORT-DIGIT(A, B, n, i). Lines 14–15 leave each pass’s result in A, ready for the next digit.
